The job interview

Anglais · Terminale

Dialogue fictif écrit pour la leçon. Les faits et les dates cités sont exacts.

Objectif

Understand and use the language of a job interview; use the present perfect continuous (I have been working…) and question tags (…, isn’t it?) in context.

Programme : Progression harmonisée Tle · Family and social life, lessons 1-3 (undergoing a job interview), lesson 5 (present perfect continuous), lesson 8 (question tags)

Lieu : A hotel in Limbe · Monday, 9 a.m.

Personnages

  • Mrs Ndive, hotel manager
  • Eric, applicant
  • The receptionist

La scène

A big hotel in Limbe is looking for a receptionist who speaks English and French. Eric has an interview this morning. — Un grand hôtel de Limbe cherche un réceptionniste parlant anglais et français. Eric a un entretien ce matin.

The receptionist : Good morning. You’re here for the interview, aren’t you? — Bonjour. Vous êtes là pour l’entretien, n’est-ce pas ?

Eric, applicant : Yes, I am. My name is Eric Ngwa. I have an appointment with Mrs Ndive at nine. — Oui. Je m’appelle Eric Ngwa. J’ai rendez-vous avec Mme Ndive à neuf heures.

Question : In “You’re here for the interview, aren’t you?”, what is “aren’t you?”

  • A question tag: a short question added to check information. (bonne réponse)
  • A mistake: it should be “are you?”.

Positive statement → negative tag: You are…, aren’t you? Negative statement → positive tag: You aren’t…, are you?

Mrs Ndive invites Eric into her office.

Mrs Ndive, hotel manager : Please sit down. Tell me about yourself. — Asseyez-vous, je vous prie. Parlez-moi de vous.

Eric, applicant : I hold a BTS in tourism. I have been working part-time in a restaurant in Buea for two years, so I have been dealing with customers every day. — J’ai un BTS en tourisme. Je travaille à temps partiel dans un restaurant de Buea depuis deux ans ; je m’occupe donc de clients tous les jours.

Present perfect continuous

have / has + been + verb-ing. It describes an action that started in the past and is still going on: “I have been working here for two years” (j’y travaille depuis deux ans). With “for” + a duration, with “since” + a starting point: “since 2024”.

Workshop · build the sentence

Put the words in order: « J’apprends l’allemand depuis septembre. »

  1. I
  2. have
  3. been
  4. learning
  5. German
  6. since
  7. September.

I have been learning German since September. Since + a starting point.

Mrs Ndive, hotel manager : Why do you want to work here? — Pourquoi voulez-vous travailler ici ?

À toi de décider : You are Eric. How do you answer?

  • “Because I need money.” — Honest, but it says nothing about the job or the hotel. The manager is not impressed.
  • “Because your hotel welcomes visitors from all over the world, and I enjoy helping people in both English and French.” — You link your skills to the job: a strong answer.
  • “I don’t know, my uncle told me to come.” — You show no motivation at all.

Mrs Ndive, hotel manager : Good. You speak French too, don’t you? — Bien. Vous parlez aussi français, n’est-ce pas ?

Eric, applicant : Yes, I do. I grew up in Douala, so I am fully bilingual. — Oui. J’ai grandi à Douala, je suis donc parfaitement bilingue.

Workshop · the right tag

Choose the correct question tag for each sentence.

  • …, isn’t it? : It is a good hotel, … ; The salary is fair, …
  • …, doesn’t she? : She speaks English, …
  • …, haven’t you? : You have worked in Buea, …

The tag repeats the auxiliary (is, does, have) and the subject pronoun.

Mrs Ndive, hotel manager : Thank you, Mr Ngwa. We will call you by Friday. — Merci, monsieur Ngwa. Nous vous appellerons d’ici vendredi.

Quiz

  1. Complete: “I have been … here for two years.” — working have been + verb-ing.
  2. “since” or “for”? “… 2024” — since Since + a starting point.
  3. Choose the right tag: “She is the manager, …?” — isn’t she Positive statement → negative tag.
  4. What does “applicant” mean? — Candidat To apply for a job: postuler.
  5. The best answer to “Why do you want to work here?” connects… — the company, the job and your skills. That is what the manager wants to hear.

À retenir

  • Present perfect continuous: have / has + been + verb-ing, for an action that started in the past and continues.
  • For + duration (for two years); since + starting point (since 2024).
  • Question tags: positive statement → negative tag (isn’t it?), negative statement → positive tag (is it?).
  • Interview vocabulary: applicant, appointment, skills, experience, to apply for, to hire.

À écrire

Write a dialogue of ten lines between a manager and an applicant for a job in a bank. Use the present perfect continuous at least twice and two question tags.
