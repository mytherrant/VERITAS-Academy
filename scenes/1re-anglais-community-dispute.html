The goat and the maize field

Anglais · Première

Dialogue fictif écrit pour la leçon. Les faits et les dates cités sont exacts.

Objectif

Understand and use the language of settling a community dispute; use conditionals (types 1, 2 and 3), unless / provided that and modals of advice and obligation in context.

Programme : Progression harmonisée 1re · Family and social life, lesson 2 (modals), lessons 7 and 9 (settling community disputes), lesson 10 (conditionals 1, 2, 3)

Lieu : A quarter in Dschang · Saturday, 10 a.m.

Personnages

  • Pa Nkwain, quarter head
  • Mr Tamfu, farmer
  • Mrs Bih, goat owner
  • Nfor, Première student

La scène

Every Saturday, Pa Nkwain, the quarter head, settles small disputes. Today, his nephew Nfor, a Première student, helps him write the report in English. — Chaque samedi, Pa Nkwain, le chef de quartier, règle les petits conflits. Aujourd’hui, son neveu Nfor, élève de Première, l’aide à rédiger le compte rendu en anglais.

Mr Tamfu arrives, angry. Mrs Bih follows him. Her goat has eaten half of his maize field. — M. Tamfu arrive, en colère. Mme Bih le suit. Sa chèvre a mangé la moitié de son champ de maïs.

Mr Tamfu, farmer : Pa, if her goat comes into my field again, I will kill it! — Pa, si sa chèvre revient dans mon champ, je la tue !

Pa Nkwain, quarter head : Calm down, my son. If you killed the goat, you would create a bigger problem. Sit down, both of you. — Calme-toi, mon fils. Si tu tuais la chèvre, tu créerais un problème plus grave. Asseyez-vous tous les deux.

Question : “If her goat comes again, I will kill it.” Which conditional is it?

  • Type 1: if + present, will + infinitive. A real, possible situation. (bonne réponse)
  • Type 2: an imaginary situation.

Yes. Tamfu thinks it can really happen again.

Mrs Bih, goat owner : I am sorry. If I had known, I would have tied it. The rope broke during the night. — Je suis désolée. Si j’avais su, je l’aurais attachée. La corde s’est cassée pendant la nuit.

The three conditionals

Type 1 (possible): If it comes again, I will kill it. Type 2 (imaginary now): If you killed it, you would create a problem. Type 3 (past, did not happen): If I had known, I would have tied it. Never “will” in the if-clause.

Workshop · build the sentence

Put the words in order: « Si j’avais une clôture, ma chèvre ne sortirait pas. »

  1. If
  2. I
  3. had
  4. a
  5. fence,
  6. my
  7. goat
  8. would
  9. not
  10. go
  11. out.

If I had a fence, my goat would not go out. Type 2: if + past, would + infinitive.

Pa Nkwain, quarter head : Mr Tamfu, how much maize have you lost? — Monsieur Tamfu, combien de maïs avez-vous perdu ?

Mr Tamfu, farmer : About two bags. That is my children’s school fees! — Environ deux sacs. Ce sont les frais de scolarité de mes enfants !

À toi de décider : Pa Nkwain asks Nfor: “What would you propose?” You are Nfor. What do you say?

  • “Mrs Bih should pay for one bag now, and she must build a fence before the rainy season.” — A fair proposal: the damage is repaired and the problem is prevented. Both neighbours listen.
  • “Mr Tamfu can kill the goat; then they are equal.” — Violence answers violence. The quarter head shakes his head.
  • “They should go to the police in town.” — Possible, but long and costly for a small dispute that the quarter can settle.

Mrs Bih, goat owner : I will pay for one bag, provided that Mr Tamfu helps me build the fence. — Je paierai un sac, à condition que M. Tamfu m’aide à construire la clôture.

Mr Tamfu, farmer : Unless she ties the goat every night, the fence will not be enough. — À moins qu’elle n’attache la chèvre chaque nuit, la clôture ne suffira pas.

Question : “Unless she ties the goat…” means:

  • If she does not tie the goat… (bonne réponse)
  • If she ties the goat…

Unless = if … not.

Workshop · which conditional?

Put each sentence in the right box.

  • Type 1 (possible) : If it rains, the maize will grow. ; If the fence breaks, we will repair it.
  • Type 2 (imaginary) : If I were the chief, I would plant more trees.
  • Type 3 (past regret) : If you had come earlier, you would have seen the goat.

Type 1: present / will. Type 2: past / would. Type 3: past perfect / would have.

Pa Nkwain, quarter head : Then it is settled. Nfor, write it down: Mrs Bih pays one bag of maize; both build the fence on Saturday. If anyone breaks this agreement, he will answer before the quarter council. — Alors c’est réglé. Nfor, écris : Mme Bih paie un sac de maïs ; tous deux construisent la clôture samedi. Si quelqu’un rompt cet accord, il en répondra devant le conseil du quartier.

Mr Tamfu and Mrs Bih shake hands. Nfor reads the report aloud in English, then in French, for the whole quarter. — M. Tamfu et Mme Bih se serrent la main. Nfor lit le compte rendu à voix haute en anglais, puis en français, pour tout le quartier.

Quiz

  1. Complete: “If it rains tomorrow, the maize … grow.” — will Type 1: if + present, will + infinitive.
  2. Complete: “If I … a fence, my goat would not go out.” — had Type 2: if + simple past.
  3. “If I had known, I … it.” — would have tied Type 3: would have + past participle.
  4. “Unless” means: — if … not Unless she ties it = if she does not tie it.
  5. A person who helps two sides to agree is a… — mediator To mediate, mediation, a mediator.

À retenir

  • Type 1: if + present → will + infinitive (possible).
  • Type 2: if + past → would + infinitive (imaginary); “if I were”.
  • Type 3: if + past perfect → would have + past participle (past regret).
  • Unless = if … not; provided (that) = only if.
  • Mediation vocabulary: dispute, to settle, to compensate, an agreement, a mediator, to shake hands.

À écrire

Write the report of the meeting (about 150 words) that Nfor reads to the quarter. Use at least one conditional of each type and “unless” or “provided that”.
